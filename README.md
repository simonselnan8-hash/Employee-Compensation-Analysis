### Project Overview
I recently worked on an Employee Compensation Analysis Dashboard using Microsoft Power BI to analyze workforce data and provide insights into employee salaries, total compensation, bonuses, industry-level expenditure, gender distribution, and yearly salary trends.

The goal of this project was to transform raw employee data into meaningful visual insights that can help management understand compensation patterns, monitor salary expenditure, and make more informed workforce planning decisions.

Through data analysis and interactive dashboard design, I explored how compensation varies across industries, how bonuses are distributed by gender, and how salary expenditure changes over time.

### Project Objectives
- Analyze workforce size and employee compensation patterns.
- Compare total compensation across different industries.
- Examine bonus distribution between male and female employees.
- Identify the maximum salary within the dataset.
- Track yearly salary expenditure from 2022 to 2026.
- Provide actionable recommendations to support compensation planning and management decisions.

### Tools and Technologies
- Microsoft Power BI – Dashboard development and data visualization.
- Data Analysis – Identifying compensation patterns and trends.
- Interactive Dashboard Design – Presenting key metrics and insights in a clear, accessible format.

### Key Dashboard Metrics
- Total Employees: 1,000

- Total Compensation: $147,783,005

## Key Insights
### 1. Compensation by Industry
The analysis revealed differences in total compensation across five industries:
- Energy: Approximately $39 million — the highest.
- Healthcare: Approximately $31.5 million.
- Education: Approximately $28 million.
- Finance: Approximately $27 million.
- Retail: Approximately $22 million — the lowest among the five industries.
Insight: The Energy industry accounts for the largest share of total compensation in the dataset. Further analysis could help determine whether this is driven by employee headcount, higher salary levels, specialized roles, or bonus payments.

### 2. Average Bonus Distribution by Gender
- The dashboard showed an almost evenly balanced distribution of bonus earnings:
- Female: Approximately $10K, representing 50.45%.
- Male: Approximately $10K, representing 49.55%.
Insight: The reported bonus distribution is nearly equal between male and female employees. However, a more detailed analysis of average bonus amounts by gender, job level, department, and performance would help assess compensation fairness more accurately.

### 3. Maximum Salary Analysis
The maximum salary recorded in the dataset was approximately $239,980.

Insight: This metric helps management understand the upper end of the salary structure and identify areas for further analysis, such as senior-level positions, highly specialized roles, and differences in compensation across industries.

### 4. Salary Expenditure by Work Year
The dashboard highlighted the following yearly salary expenditure:
- 2022: $28.6 million
- 2023: $28.4 million
- 2024: $27.2 million
- 2025: $28.0 million
- 2026: $25.4 million

Insight: Salary expenditure remained relatively stable between 2022 and 2025, with some fluctuations. In 2026, expenditure declined to $25.4 million, the lowest figure in the period analyzed.

This decline presents an opportunity to investigate changes in employee headcount, recruitment, staff turnover, salary adjustments, or the completeness of the 2026 data.

### Recommendations
Based on the dashboard findings, I recommend the following:
 1. Investigate the 2026 Salary Decline: Review workforce size, recruitment activity, employee turnover, and compensation changes to understand the reduction in salary expenditure.
 2. Review Energy Industry Compensation: Examine headcount, job roles, salary levels, and bonus structures to understand why the Energy industry has the highest total compensation.
 3. Monitor Compensation Fairness: Continue comparing bonuses and salaries across gender groups, while accounting for job roles, seniority, experience, and performance.
 4. Improve Workforce Budgeting: Use historical salary trends and employee data to support future compensation forecasting and budget planning.
 5. Strengthen Data-Driven Decision-Making: Regularly monitor key compensation KPIs to identify unusual changes and support evidence-based management decisions.

### Business Value
This project demonstrates how Power BI can transform employee data into an interactive dashboard that helps organizations:
- Monitor workforce and compensation KPIs.

- Understand salary expenditure trends.

- Identify areas that require further investigation.
